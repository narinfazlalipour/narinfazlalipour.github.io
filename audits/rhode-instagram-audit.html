rhode skin is the DTC skincare brand founded by Hailey Rhode Bieber in 2022 — and in three years, it has become one of the most culturally dominant beauty brands on Instagram. At 5 million followers, ~$212M in trailing net sales, and a landmark ~$1B acquisition by e.l.f. Beauty (May 2025), rhode's Instagram presence is a case study in celebrity-to-commerce execution. The "glazed donut" aesthetic is instantly recognizable, every product launch is a cultural event, and the brand's influencer program (32,700 sponsored posts in 12 months) is the largest in its competitive set — 2× Summer Fridays, 6× Glossier.

But growth at this tier demands more than launch spikes. rhode's Instagram underperforms its business momentum in three critical areas: community depth (amber 62/100 — high volume, low attachment), format diversity (68/100 — overwhelmingly product-photo-and-Reel, with almost no educational, founder-story, or community-building content), and between-launch content cadence (72/100 — the feed goes quiet between drops and the algorithm penalizes the silence). The opportunity is structural: rhode has the hardest thing — a beloved product and a founder people genuinely want to watch. What's missing is the content architecture to convert that goodwill into recurring engagement, not just recurring purchases.

Diagnosis: rhode built a conversion machine that looks like a brand. The next growth tier — from 5M to 10M — requires building a brand that happens to convert. That means feed presence between launches, founder-as-storyteller content, and an Instagram-native community layer that TikTok can't replicate. The e.l.f. acquisition gives rhode the operational runway to make this shift without revenue pressure. The window is now.

3. Strengths

Category-defining visual identity

The "glazed donut" aesthetic — dewy skin, soft lighting, minimalist packaging — is now a beauty-industry reference point. rhode didn't borrow a look; it created one. Every post is instantly attributable, even without a logo.

Celebrity founder as authentic product user

Hailey Bieber is not a paid ambassador — she is the brand. Her skin IS the product demo. This authenticity is rare in celebrity beauty and gives rhode a trust signal most founder-led brands can't replicate: the founder's face is the clinical trial.

Dominant influencer program

32,700 sponsored posts across 16,900 unique creators in 12 months — 2× Summer Fridays, 6× Glossier. The program is always-on, globally distributed, and heavily skewed toward micro-influencers (39% under 5K avg. views), creating an authenticity flywheel that paid media can't match.

Scarcity-engine launches

Every drop is a cultural event. Limited-edition Peptide Lip Tints, waitlist-only products, and "sold out" badges create FOMO that drives conversion rates few DTC brands achieve. The launch playbook is polished to a science.

Pristine algorithmic trust signal

A 0.01% following-to-follower ratio (513 following at 5M followers) is virtually perfect. Instagram's trust classifier sees maximum authenticity — every post gets full algorithmic reach window. This is a structural advantage most large accounts have already compromised.

Product-market fit validated at scale

$212M in net sales from ~15 SKUs, DTC-only. Allure Best of Beauty awards in 2022, 2024, and 2025. The products work, the reviews are genuine, and the price point ($16–$50) hits the accessible-prestige sweet spot.

4. Weaknesses

Feast-or-famine content cadence

The feed spikes around launches and goes quiet in between. Instagram's algorithm penalizes inconsistency — each dormant stretch resets reach velocity and forces the next post to rebuild from a cold start. A 5M-follower account should never feel like it "went dark."

Transactional community, not an owned audience

rhode's influencer program is vast but shallow — only 2% of creators post in 3+ months. The brand has a transactional relationship with its community (product → purchase → next product) rather than a content-driven one (follow → engage → belong). When the launch calendar slows, so does the audience.

Instagram is secondary to TikTok

57.4% of sponsored content lives on TikTok. Instagram gets the reposts. This is strategically rational — TikTok drives discovery — but Instagram is where the brand's commerce lives. A secondary-platform Instagram strategy caps the conversion potential of 5M followers.

No educational or founder-story content

The feed is almost exclusively product photography, launch announcements, and celebrity-face content. There's no barrier-education content, no ingredient deep-dives, no Hailey-on-camera explaining why she formulated Barrier Butter the way she did. The product is the hero — but the hero needs a story.

Carousel underuse

Carousels achieve the highest per-impression engagement on Instagram (1.92% avg vs. 1.45% for video), and they're the format most likely to be saved and revisited. rhode posts 1–2 carousels per week — below the 2–3 benchmark for a 5M-tier brand. Educational, comparison, and routine-building content is carousel-native and currently absent.

Stories as afterthought, not engagement layer

At 3–5 Stories/day, rhode is leaving the platform's highest-intimacy surface underleveraged. Stories drive DM replies, poll engagement, link clicks, and the "close friends" signal that feeds the algorithm. A brand with rhode's product pipeline should run 8–12 Stories/day during launch weeks and 5–8 on off weeks.

8. Engagement Growth Strategy

1

Build a between-launch content architecture

rhode needs content series that run independently of the launch calendar — recurring formats that train the audience to check the feed even when nothing is dropping. Three series: "Barrier School" (educational carousels on skin barrier science), "Formula Files" (ingredient deep-dives — why squalane, why peptides, why PGA), and "Hailey's Routine" (weekly Story series showing real product use, not polished campaign footage). These create algorithmic continuity between launches and give the feed a heartbeat.

2

Launch a founder-Live program

Instagram Live is the platform's highest-priority format in 2026 and rhode has the single best Live asset in beauty: Hailey Bieber talking about skincare, unrehearsed. A monthly "Skin Check-In" Live — 15 minutes, Hailey + a formulator or derm, audience Q&A — would drive DM engagement, real-time comment velocity, and the kind of community intimacy no influencer post can replicate. Schedule it the week after every launch to capture peak attention.

3

Double carousel output with save-worthy content

Move from 1–2 carousels/week to 3–4. Every carousel should be designed for the save, not the double-tap. Routine builders ("Your Barrier Restore Morning"), ingredient comparisons ("Peptide Lip Treatment vs. Lip Shape — when to use which"), and myth-busters ("No, glazed skin isn't oily skin") are all carousel-native formats that compound in the algorithm long after posting. Every save extends the post's half-life by weeks.

4

Layer IG-native content on top of TikTok repurposing

TikTok-first makes sense for discovery, but Instagram content should be designed for Instagram behaviors — saves, shares to Close Friends, DM sends. Re-edit TikTok Reels for Instagram pacing (slower first 3 seconds, text overlays for sound-off viewers), add save-CTA captions, and pair every repurposed Reel with an Instagram-native carousel that deepens the topic.

5

Activate Stories as a daily presence

Target 5–8 Stories/day on off weeks, 10–12 during launch weeks. Include: morning routine snippet (product in use), one poll ("Which lip tint shade for summer?"), one question sticker ("What's your skin barrier struggling with right now?"), one BTS clip (packaging design, lab visit, Hailey in a fitting), and one link-out (blog post, product page, press feature). Stories are rhode's lowest-cost, highest-intimacy surface and they're currently running at half-capacity.

10. Quick Wins — This Week

1. Post a "What's In My Routine" carousel today

Simple, high-save, zero-production carousel. Slide 1: "My barrier restore routine — morning edition." Slides 2–6: one product per slide with one sentence on why that product, in that order. Last slide: "Save this for your next restock." This is the highest-effort-to-engagement ratio post rhode can make this week — no shoot required, existing product imagery, save-optimized.

Friday: "Drop a 🍩 if you've been glazed today." Polls are 2-tap interactions with outsized algorithmic weight — they signal active audience investment. Three polls this week, every week.

3. Pin a "Start Here" carousel to the profile grid

rhode's profile currently has no pinned orientation content — a 5M-follower brand should make the first grid row a navigation layer. Create a 6-slide carousel: "New here? Start with these four." Pin it to position 1–3 in the grid. Update it quarterly as the product line evolves. This converts profile visits (tens of thousands/day) into guided product discovery instead of passive scrolling.

13. Road to 6.5M

Phase 1: Foundation (Days 1–30) — "Turn the Feed On"

Target: 5M → 5.3M. Actions: (1) Launch one educational carousel series — "Barrier School" — and publish 4 episodes in 30 days. (2) Increase Stories to 5–7/day with daily polls and question stickers. (3) Pin a "Start Here" orientation carousel. (4) Begin replying to first 30 comments within 4 hours of every post. (5) Schedule the first monthly "Skin Check-In" Live for Day 28. Success signal: carousel save rate rises above 3%, Story replies increase measurably, and the feed stops feeling "dark" between launches.

Phase 2: Acceleration (Days 31–60) — "Build the Rhythms"

Target: 5.3M → 5.7M. Actions: (1) Add second carousel series — "Formula Files" — alternating weeks with Barrier School. (2) Run 2 Live sessions/month (founder Q&A + formulator deep-dive). (3) Launch a weekly "Routine Reel" series — Hailey on camera (not just modeled), walking through a product application with real technique and real talk. (4) Begin UGC repost rhythm — 2–3 community posts/week in Stories, creating social proof flywheel. (5) Test save-CTA captions systematically (A/B "save for later" vs. "bookmark this routine"). Success signal: engagement rate ticks above 4%, Live viewership establishes a baseline, community UGC pipeline activates.

Phase 3: Compound (Days 61–90) — "Own the Category"

Target: 5.7M → 6.5M. Actions: (1) All four content pillars running simultaneously: educational carousels (2×/week), founder-story Reels (1×/week), community-UGC Stories (daily), Live programming (2×/month). (2) Launch "The Glazed Podcast" — a monthly Instagram Live that doubles as a podcast episode, featuring Hailey in conversation with derms, formulators, and beauty founders. (3) Integrate e.l.f. Beauty distribution announcement content — retail shelf photography, "now at [retailer]" Reels, mass-accessibility storytelling. (4) Full cross-platform content engine: IG-native content feeds TikTok, TikTok discoveries feed IG, both drive to DTC. Success signal: 6.5M followers, 4.5%+ engagement rate, and — critically — the feed feels alive between launches with recurring series the audience anticipates.
